# Outlet access and reuse permissions

## Scope and reading rule

This note evaluates five candidate sources for a VaporStats/Wayfinder research integration: IGN, Kotaku, The Verge, BBC gaming coverage, and Eurogamer. It separates:

- **technical access**: a page, feed, image URL, or robots file was reachable;
- **publisher permission**: an official term, feed rule, embed control, or licence expressly permits a use;
- **restriction**: an official source expressly prohibits or conditions a use; and
- **unknown**: the reviewed public sources do not answer the question.

A reachable page, RSS feed, metadata tag, image URL, or permissive `robots.txt` rule is not a copyright or trademark licence. A robots directive is recorded as an access signal only. This is a planning note, not a legal determination; fair-use/fair-dealing or other exceptions are jurisdiction- and fact-dependent.

## Executive finding

All five have real games coverage and at least some technically reachable feed or category surface. None of the reviewed public sources gives VaporStats a blanket right to fetch, retain, excerpt, summarize, analyze, host, or hotlink outlet editorial text or images. The Verge's Vox licensing guidance does provide narrow permission for integrated share/embed tools, headline/title plus original-link sharing, and tightly constrained review quotations; those permissions are not a blanket feed, article, image, or logo licence. The lowest-risk interim surface is **publisher name + canonical outbound link + minimal factual metadata that is independently available**, with no outlet article images or logos. Article excerpts, attributed summaries, stored article bodies, image hosting, and outlet-brand icons should wait for a written licence or a source-specific permission that covers those exact uses.

- **IGN/Ziff Davis**: feeds are openly listed and ask feed users to cite IGN.com, but Ziff Davis terms separately prohibit commercial use, scraping, analysis/data-set creation, AI/TDM, and significant electronic storage. IGN's correction policy says it generally updates rather than unpublishes and does not agree to ordinary takedown requests. Feed availability is not a republication licence.
- **Kotaku**: an RSS endpoint and games archive are reachable, but current terms grant only personal, non-commercial use and prohibit automated scrapers. The robots file blocks `/embed/` and named AI/scraper agents. The privacy policy's retention language concerns personal data, not a right to retain Kotaku's editorial articles.
- **The Verge**: games/RSS endpoints are reachable; full-text RSS is a subscriber benefit and premium entries are preview-only for non-subscribers. Vox's licensing policy permits integrated share/embed tools, canonical headline/title links, and limited review quotations under stated conditions, while terms prohibit general extraction, copying, framing, distribution, and commercial use. Subscriber access is not a syndication licence.
- **BBC**: its official rules are unusually explicit: business use of RSS or metadata requires permission/licensing (possibly a fee); article content, images, logos, and computer analysis require permission; an official embed button is required for embeddable content and the player must not be altered or extracted.
- **Eurogamer**: its current About page says Gamer Network Limited is an IGN Entertainment company and Ziff Davis subsidiary; news/review feeds are listed, but terms limit use to private, non-commercial personal use and prohibit third-party display/copy/reuse without written permission. Its robots file expressly prohibits automated scraping, TDM, AI/ML, datasets, and commercial purposes without Ziff Davis permission.

## Dimension-by-dimension implementation boundary

These are planning guardrails derived from the matrix, not permissions:

1. **Fetch**: use only a publisher-provided feed/API or ordinary page request that the current robots/terms allow. Identify the crawler, rate-limit it, cache only what a written licence allows, and stop on a disallow or access failure. Never bypass HTTP blocks, paywalls, bot checks, or `/embed/`/private paths.
2. **Retain**: assume no right to store article bodies, images, thumbnails, or icons. A future agreement must specify fields, cache/retention duration, update cadence, deletion/takedown propagation, backups, and whether derived summaries survive source deletion.
3. **Excerpt and summarize**: do not ship outlet article excerpts or attributed summaries based on retained article text without source-specific written permission. A title/date/author/link record is not an excerpt. The Verge's Vox licensing policy is a narrow exception for review quotes: no more than three sentences from a review in the past year, in context, with prominent attribution; confirm the current policy and rights holder before using it. Fair-use/fair-dealing exceptions are jurisdiction- and fact-dependent and are not a blanket product policy.
4. **Analyze**: do not run full-text clustering, embeddings, TDM, or model training on outlet articles under the public terms reviewed. Ziff Davis and BBC expressly prohibit or condition analysis/TDM/AI; the other sources prohibit the automated extraction/copying needed to build such a corpus.
5. **Embed**: use only a publisher-provided embed control where the publisher's rules permit it. BBC expressly requires its embed button and forbids extraction/modification. Vox permits integrated share tools/embed codes where available, not an article-page iframe or extracted copy. Do not iframe outlet article pages as a substitute for permission. YouTube is a separate platform path covered by its own terms and API policies.
6. **Display images and icons**: hotlinking is not a licence. Avoid outlet article images, screenshots, thumbnails, logos, and favicons. Use plain-text source names and links or project-owned neutral marks. Vox's policy permits a review logo/badge only with a qualifying review quote or promotional fact and never as a standalone logo; all other images require a case-by-case licence. If a future licence authorizes an icon, store the authorized asset and follow its attribution/brand constraints rather than copying a page asset opportunistically.
7. **Attribution and traffic**: preserve canonical URL, source name, title, publication time, author/byline when available, and an obvious outbound link. IGN expressly requests `IGN.com`; BBC specifies exact nearby attribution/link rules for RSS; Eurogamer requires proprietary notices to remain. Vox permits a headline/title link with the original URL and imposes prominent attribution for permitted review quotes. No other reviewed source supplies a sufficient general-purpose attribution licence.
8. **Takedown**: a contract should name an operational contact, source URL/ID, notification format, removal deadline, backup purge, and treatment of summaries/embeddings. IGN's corrections policy generally rejects ordinary unpublishing/takedown requests, so an agreement must specify how corrections and source changes affect derived data. Public contacts found here are Ziff Davis `legal@ziffdavis.com`/`licensing@ziffdavis.com`, Vox `copyright@voxmedia.com`, BBC permission/licensing forms and `carriage.enquiries@bbc.co.uk`, Kotaku's legal department, and Eurogamer `contact@eurogamer.net`.
9. **Metadata**: feeds and HTML expose varying title/date/link/byline/image metadata, but metadata can itself be licensed (BBC says so explicitly). Record only fields covered by the chosen source agreement; do not assume Open Graph images, author avatars, logos, or category labels are reusable.

## Shortlist assessment

All five are credible candidates for a discovery study because their first-party pages demonstrate ongoing games coverage. They are not equally viable as data sources:

- **Most tractable for a permission conversation**: IGN and Eurogamer have a common Ziff Davis licensing path and clearly documented feeds, but the current Ziff restrictions make written permission necessary for anything beyond link-oriented metadata.
- **Potentially tractable only with a clear feed licence**: BBC has explicit RSS/metadata licensing routes and strict embed/branding rules; it is the clearest source for a formal licence conversation, not for unlicensed ingestion.
- **Narrow public sharing path, but not article ingestion**: The Verge has explicit link/integrated-embed permissions and a tightly constrained review-quote/badge policy. It remains unsuitable for article-body retention, broad summaries, image reuse, or full-text RSS ingestion without a separate agreement.
- **Technically reachable but rights-unclear**: Kotaku exposes feeds/games surfaces, but the reviewed sources grant no required product rights. The Verge's subscription full-text RSS is access-controlled and should not be treated as syndication.

This is not an adopted source-selection decision. The remaining decision is whether to admit only sources with written permission for each requested use, or to start with a link-and-minimal-metadata mode that does not reuse outlet article content or branding.

## Parent-supplied non-outlet context

These sources are legal/platform context, not permissions from any outlet:

- [YouTube official embed help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780?hl=en): official embed player is available subject to YouTube terms/API policies; uploaders can disable embedding; privacy-enhanced `youtube-nocookie` is available; a `Referer` is required in the documented cases; embedding does not itself grant transcript-extraction rights.
- [YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms?hl=en&override_hl=1): the retrieved regional/versioned text permits the embeddable player through enabled Service features and prohibits unauthorized automated access. The retrieved page was a 2022 BR-English courtesy translation, so the latest applicable version must be checked before implementation.
- [U.S. Copyright Office fair use](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/): fair use is a four-factor, fact-specific analysis; noncommercial purpose is not automatically safe and there is no fixed safe word count.
- [UK government copyright exceptions](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright): criticism/quotation may qualify with acknowledgement; text-and-data mining exceptions have lawful-access and noncommercial-research conditions. A public hobby/project site does not automatically satisfy them.

## Source limitations

- Public terms can change, may apply to a corporate family rather than one brand, and may not cover every image, freelancer contribution, syndication deal, or third-party embed on an article.
- A robots file reports a publisher's requested crawler behavior; it does not grant or waive copyright, trademark, contract, or privacy rights.
- This pass did not attempt authenticated feeds, paid subscriptions, private APIs, or access-control circumvention. IGN main-site 403s remain an evidence limitation. The Verge ownership/closing and Eurogamer contracting details should be confirmed with the current licensing contact before any agreement.
